Combining this desire with something "innovative" and practically useful seemed a decent approach, since a **fast and efficient** and **easily implemented** portable crypto cipher design was required for low-power embedded systems, even when this may clash with a few interests and ideas among those who consider Bitcoin some reasonable implementation of "crypto". Rather not confuse those issues, since the design-rationale of symmetric [SCRAM](https://codeberg.org/aggi/linux-tcc/commit/ede4ba1022e571cca61f35cf639b48b5d972c141) crypto is fundementally different. And for that matter asymmetric cryptography can be discussed separately and is not a subject of this thesis.

The main criteria for a ["proven cipher design"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5/2) was processing speed and simplicity, while preventing known-plaintext key-recovery attacks.[^ATTACK] The metric to evaluate any [symmetric cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_cipher), [cryptographic hash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_hash_function) or [PRNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorandom_number_generator) is **maximum brute-force resistence** and **maximum known-plaintext key-recovery attack resistance** with **minimum memory/cpu resource consumption** during regular operation. Such resistence can be maximized **without complicating or slowing down** any cipher design, and without increasing processing-rounds or increasing memory consumption. Furthermore a decent cipher design does **not** depend on vendor-specific proprietary hardware extensions for instruction-level-parallelization (super-scalar ILP for example [AES-NI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AES-NI)) to achieve optimal performance and security. And ideally any cipher-design built upon a mathematical rationale which was studied in detail for decades. [Linear Feeback Shift Registers (LFSR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear-feedback_shift_register) were well known subject of technical computer science, considered fast and simple with decent cryptographic properties ("randomness"), but were **not recommended** for encryption, because known-plaintext key recovery methods existed.

[^ATTACK]: [Breaking NATO Radio Encryption](https://media.ccc.de/v/38c3-breaking-nato-radio-encryption) showcases how a complicated cipher design remains prone to key-recovery attacks which easily could be prevented with a much simpler and efficient cipher design.

Nonetheless, the SCRAM cipher design relying on XORSHIFT instruction with [barrel-shifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_shifter) ensures both decent cryptographic properties and efficiency, supported with almost any CPU nowadays. Furthermore doing one "round" interleaved bit-wise permutation on 32bit/64bit datatypes seems to saturate data-bus more efficiently, instead of multiple "rounds" of byte-wise permutations common with any of the other typical cipher designs such as [RC4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC4) and similar. Hence SCRAM cipher design is almost ~10x faster and more efficient than most known publicly available symmetric block ciphers implemented in software. Of cause SCRAM cipher design **must not** be prone to known-plaintext key-recovery attacks at all even with least optimal [ECB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_codebook) mode of operation. However LFSR weakness of being prone to [known-plaintext key-recovery attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Known-plaintext_attack) had to be addressed when building upon their design rationale. By interleaving multiple LFSR and utilizing **dynamic generator-polynoms** derived from secret key and plaintext input, any known-plaintext key-recovery is blocked with a **non**-[primitive recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_recursive_function). To conduct a known-plaintext attack outside regular symmetric operation of SCRAM cipher an equation system for the LFSR generator-polynoms must be defined, however such are dynamic and alter their internal state continously. Finally then the cipher-algorithm itself is unknown and had to be guessed, which polynomes it could have been, which produces an unresolvable non-primitive recursion. The [computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation_theory) properties of non-primitive recursion are easier demonstrated with the [Ackermann-Function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ackermann_Function) which it is unknown with if any such algorithm would terminate anytime, or not, because not at least the [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_complexity_theory) could be estimated for a [KPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Known_plaintext_attack). Finally LFSR can be utilized with symmetric encryption! Both a 64bit and 32bit data-type variant were implemented, permitting either stream-mode or any block-mode of operation, which can be tuned to any desired block size. The default heavily increased 512Byte/4096Bit block-size with SCRAM cipher seems to benefit run-time performance too, instead of processing and chaining smaller chunks of 128bits (with AES-128 for example).

A symmetric cipher, hash, PRNG were implemented and tested against various APIs of dm-crypt, loopdev, dropbear-ssh, ipsec stack and in principle this cipher design is broadly portable towards any linux2, linux5, and userspace utilities.
