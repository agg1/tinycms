# Results
Anyway, a most important aspect, **WHY** Telekom might have revoked their branding from the university, that was missed completely with the [public announcement made](https://www.telekom.com/de/konzern/details/hochschule-fuer-telekommunikation-leipzig-1023234), probably because it would have shed some light onto **responsibilities**, if terminating a University operating with a particular branding was considered a failure: that was the lectures and university schedule itself, of **what** was being lectured, **how** lectures were given, and the time schedule for this.

With their last announcement, Telekom mentions half a dozen bachelor courses and lectures to pass almost all of them at once in 4 years total with an exam for a final "international master" degree. Year 2001 at the time when i began studying, that was bundled into two major branches of studies "only": the traditional electronics/signal-processing degree (Nachrichtentechnik) and the newly introduced Telekommunikations-Informatik intersecting with each other, and the latter it was i had chosen. With either of those a student could graduate with what was known "engineering diploma" academic degree, another PhD graduation thereafter. A professor's degree was granted, when officially permitted giving lectures to students, in parallel to working in the industry often. What's advertized as "international master" nowadays, that formerly merely had been Scientists and Engineers with several academic degrees granted each, doing research, and working in the industry. Anyway, i doubt anything relevant changed since year 2005 up until year 2022 with any of the various "transformations". Hence when referring to the art and style of management and lectures at such university, it's save to say at least some half-way decent education was received from academic university staff hired from accross entire Germany, for the next generation of students to graduate with an internationally recognized academic degree.

However the studies were structured, the Telekommunikations-Informatik one in particular had combined several different branches of computer science, engineering, electronics, all at once, which i'll summarize with their German designation for sake of clarity:

1. Common basics of maths and physics
2. Technische Informatik (technical computer science, with digital circuit design, assembler programming, c++ programming)
3. Wirtschaftsinformatik (business computer science, with Java programming, Oracle databases, a little finance mathematics)
4. Theoretische Informatik (computer science theory, for encoding schemes, cryptography, artificial intelligence and a little related mathematics)
5. Angewandte Informatik Telekom (applied computer science, common focus on networking basics and specific focus on **both** internet Cisco router topics **and** on telco backbone protocols such as ss7/isdn/etc)
6. Medieninformatik (media computing/creativity, web design, adobe premiere)
7. Elektrotechnik/Signalverarbeitung (notable amounts of electronics, signal processing, signals and systems, analog, and DSP)

Contracting with TelekomHQ as "high potential" required _all_ of those courses in 4 years, to pass all of them more or less succesfully including a traineeship and a diploma thesis a half year each.

Anyway, as a consequence of all of this done in parallel all at once, digital circuit design lectures (VHDL etc), Motorola Assembler programming, Java programming, Oracle Databases, Internet and telco backbone protocol tracing, UMTS signaling were combined into a coherent unit of god-like wisdom.
